intro: |-

  I have always been fascinated by time (physical and subjective) and how we can perceive and conceptualize something so elusive, and yet essential to interaction with the environment.

  I defended my PhD in May 2022 at Aix-Marseille University, where my work focused on the representation of temporal order, in relation with the *mental timeline* (i.e., where durations or temporal order are mapped from left to right). Drawing upon theoretical frameworks such as neural reuse and Hebbian learning, we investigated the functional role of the sensorimotor system in temporal cognition. To address this question, we developed experimental protocols combining behavioral measures (e.g., movement initiation times, mouse tracking, eye-tracking) with large-scale online studies involving more than a thousand participants. Overall, this work suggested that the sensorimotor system plays a functional role in the processing of temporal order.

  After my PhD, I joined Virginie van Wassenhove’s group at NeuroSpin (CEA, Paris-Saclay) as a postdoctoral researcher involved in the [EXPERIENCE Project](https://experience-project.eu). During this postdoctoral work, we examined how subjective duration is shaped by both environmental constraints and representational structure. In one line of research, we combined immersive virtual reality, EEG, and multivariate analyses to investigate how spatial environments influence duration production and its neural dynamics. In another line of research, we used behavioral similarity judgments, EEG recordings, and representational similarity analysis to study the geometry of duration representations. Together, these projects aimed to move beyond a purely unidimensional view of time and to characterize how subjective durations are organized at behavioral and neural levels.

  Since 2025, I have been affiliated with the Laboratoire de Psychologie et NeuroCognition (LPNC, Université Grenoble Alpes, CNRS), working with [Nathan Faivre](https://nfaivre.netlify.app/#head), and I am currently conducting research at Wake Forest University School of Medicine with Dr. Kenneth Kishida. This work extends my previous research on temporal cognition toward the temporal dynamics of conscious experience. In this context, we investigate how conscious experiences unfold over time, and how their perceived duration relates to electrophysiological and electrochemical signals.

  I am now pursuing this research through a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Global Postdoctoral Fellowship. The project focuses on the multiscale mechanisms of subjective time and temporal distortions, combining behavioral methods, surface and intracranial electrophysiology, and voltammetry. More specifically, the goal is to examine how neural dynamics and dopaminergic fluctuations may contribute to duration perception and its distortions.

  More broadly, my research lies at the interface between cognitive psychology, cognitive neuroscience, and computational approaches. Across projects, I am interested in how temporal experiences are represented, structured, and transformed, and in how different levels of analysis — behavior, neural activity, representational geometry, and neurochemical signals — can be combined to study subjective time. This research program is still developing, and my aim is to build it progressively through collaborative, methodologically rigorous, and open scientific practices.
